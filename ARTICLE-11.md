## Article 11 — Amendments

### 11.1 Proposal

An amendment to this Constitution may be proposed by:

1. a written petition supported by at least one-third (1/3) of the Executive Committee; or
2. a written petition supported by at least one-third (1/3) of General Members in good standing.

All proposed amendments shall be submitted in writing to the General Secretary and circulated to the General Members before the meeting at which the amendment is considered.
### 11.2 Deliberation

Any proposed amendment shall be circulated to all General Members in good standing at least seven (7) days before the General Assembly meeting at which it is to be considered.

### 11.3 Approval

An amendment shall require approval by a two-thirds (2/3) majority of General Members present and voting at a General Assembly meeting.

An amendment shall not take effect if it conflicts with applicable University or Departmental rules or policies.

### 11.4 Effective Date and Record

An approved amendment shall take effect immediately upon its approval by the General Assembly, unless the amendment itself specifies a later effective date. The amendment shall be incorporated into the official text of this Constitution, together with a record of its approval date and nature of the change.

Unless expressly stated otherwise, an amendment shall not retroactively affect actions, decisions, appointments, elections, or disciplinary matters completed before its effective date.

### 11.5 Limits on Amendments

No amendment shall:

1. extend the term of a sitting Executive Committee or officer beyond the limits established under Clause 5.2;
2. retroactively validate an act that was invalid under the Constitution at the time it was taken; or
3. alter the outcome of an election or disciplinary matter that has already been finally decided.
